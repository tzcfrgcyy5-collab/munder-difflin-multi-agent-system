MUNDER DIFFLIN PAPER COMPANY MULTI-AGENT SYSTEM
Design, Evaluation and Reflection Report

1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW

This project implements a four-agent inventory, quoting and sales workflow using
the smolagents framework and an SQLite business database. The architecture stays
below the five-agent limit and separates responsibilities so that pricing cannot
change stock, inventory management cannot finalize customer sales, and the sales
agent cannot bypass feasibility checks.

The system combines an LLM-driven smolagents manager with deterministic Python
rules inside the worker tools. The LLM manager interprets each intermediate result
and dynamically decides which named worker agent to invoke next. Deterministic tool
logic protects financial and inventory transactions from free-form model output.
All inputs, delegation messages and customer responses remain text based.

2. AGENT ARCHITECTURE AND DECISION PROCESS

Customer Inquiry Orchestrator Agent
- Receives the customer request and dataset context.
- Uses parse_customer_request_tool to extract dates, quantities and exact catalog names.
- Is implemented as a smolagents ToolCallingAgent powered by the configured LLM.
- Receives the three workers through the framework's managed_agents parameter.
- Inspects parsing and worker results to decide which worker agent is required next.
- Skips all workers for unsupported items and skips Quoting and Sales when Inventory
  reports an impossible order.
- Produces the final customer-safe explanation.

Inventory Agent
- Owns inventory checks, supplier lead-time assessment and reordering decisions.
- Checks projected stock at the requested deadline rather than only current stock.
- Calculates shortages, verifies supplier arrival dates and checks whether the
  combined replenishment purchase is affordable.
- Creates stock-order transactions only after every requested line is feasible.

Quoting Agent
- Owns customer pricing and bulk discounts.
- Searches historical quotes using the customer's job/event context.
- Calculates a consistent catalog-based subtotal and applies a transparent volume
  discount of 2%, 5%, 7%, 10% or 12% at defined thresholds.
- Returns the quote total, saving and history-use information to the orchestrator.

Sales Agent
- Owns final stock verification and sale completion.
- Rechecks every line at the planned fulfillment date.
- Runs a private financial health check before recording transactions.
- Records a sales transaction for every fulfilled product line.

The safe business policy is Inventory -> Quote -> Sale, but this sequence is not
hard-coded through direct Python function calls. It is supplied to the LLM manager
as orchestration guidance. During its .run() loop, the manager chooses and executes
the required managed agent based on each observed result. Consequently, a request
may invoke no worker, Inventory only, or all three workers. A quote is generated only
after feasibility, and revenue is recorded only after final verification.

3. TOOLS AND STARTER HELPER FUNCTIONS

inventory_assessment_tool
- get_all_inventory: obtains the current company-wide inventory snapshot.
- get_stock_level: calculates projected stock for each exact catalog item.
- get_supplier_delivery_date: checks whether shortages can arrive by the deadline.
- get_cash_balance: verifies that required replenishment is affordable.
- create_transaction: records approved supplier stock orders.

quote_generation_tool
- search_quote_history: retrieves comparable historical quotes by customer/event
  context before the pricing decision is finalized.
- The supplied paper_supplies catalog provides exact unit prices.

finalize_sale_tool
- get_stock_level: performs the final fulfillment-date stock check.
- generate_financial_report: performs a private business health check.
- create_transaction: records completed sales and reduces available inventory.

parse_customer_request_tool (Orchestrator)
- Normalizes natural-language product descriptions to exact catalog item names.
- Extracts quantities and the required delivery deadline for routing decisions.

The supplied generate_sample_inventory and init_database functions remain part of
database initialization. Every helper explicitly required by the rubric is called
inside at least one framework tool.

4. INVENTORY, REORDERING AND FULFILLMENT RULES

The system first checks projected stock through the delivery deadline. When stock
is insufficient, it calculates the exact shortage. Supplier lead time is based on
the supplied helper: same day for 10 or fewer units, one day for 11-100, four days
for 101-1000, and seven days for quantities above 1000.

An order is rejected when any requested item is outside the catalog, the supplier
cannot meet the customer deadline, the replenishment cannot be afforded, or final
stock verification fails. Rejected orders receive a reason and do not create a
sales transaction. Feasible reorders are scheduled for the supplier arrival date,
and the customer sale is scheduled when all lines are available.

5. QUOTING STRATEGY

The quote starts with the supplied catalog unit prices and a consistent commercial
markup. Similar historical quotes are retrieved to ground the decision in prior
company activity. Volume discounts encourage larger orders while remaining
consistent and explainable:

- Fewer than 500 units: 2%
- 500-999 units: 5%
- 1,000-4,999 units: 7%
- 5,000-9,999 units: 10%
- 10,000 or more units: 12%

Customer responses show requested items, total price, discount and saving, whether
historical quotes were available, and the promised delivery date. They never expose
cash balance, internal asset totals, purchase budgets, profit margin or system errors.

6. EVALUATION RESULTS

The complete 20-row quote_requests_sample.csv dataset was processed in request-date
order. The resulting test_results.csv contains one response for every request.

Observed results:
- Total requests evaluated: 20
- Successfully fulfilled orders: 7
- Unfulfilled/rejected orders: 13
- Rows showing a cash-balance change after chronological processing: 6
- Required minimums: 3 fulfilled, 1 unfulfilled, and 3 cash-balance changes

Examples of successful behavior include normalizing variants such as "A4 glossy
paper" to "Glossy paper," applying volume discounts, reordering feasible shortages,
and scheduling delivery within the stated deadline. Rejections included unsupported
products such as balloons, cardboard and tickets, as well as requests whose supplier
arrival date fell after the customer deadline.

The strongest aspects were genuine LLM-managed delegation, safe database mutation,
clear division of agent duties, full-dataset coverage, exact catalog-name
normalization and transparent customer responses. The system exceeded all
quantitative evaluation thresholds: fulfilled orders were more than double the
minimum and rejected orders clearly demonstrated constraint recognition.

7. LIMITATIONS AND FURTHER IMPROVEMENTS

Improvement 1: Replace rule-based product normalization with a maintained synonym
table plus semantic matching. The current parser intentionally rejects ambiguous
products; semantic matching with a confidence threshold could suggest alternatives
without silently substituting the wrong item.

Improvement 2: Add reservations and order identifiers. Stock is currently represented
through dated transactions. A separate reservations table would prevent conflicts
when several accepted orders compete for the same future delivery and would allow
cancellation or amendment workflows.

Improvement 3: Improve pricing intelligence. Historical quotes could be normalized by
product mix, quantity, recency and customer segment, then used to recommend a bounded
price range instead of only providing contextual evidence.

Improvement 4: Add proactive business advice. A reporting agent could analyze demand,
stockout frequency, supplier performance and margins, then recommend revised reorder
points and safety-stock levels without exposing sensitive figures to customers.

8. CONCLUSION

The four-agent design provides a modular and auditable workflow from inquiry through
inventory planning, quoting and final sale. It uses all mandatory database helpers,
honors supplier and inventory constraints, records financial changes, rejects
impossible requests, and explains every customer-facing outcome without disclosing
sensitive internal business information.
